2. HOW PRICES WORK TECHNICALLY (implemented)
--------------------------------------------
- One source of truth on the server: lib/billing/providers.ts → providerPolicy(country).
  US → Dodo, USD, existing prices. India → Razorpay, INR, RAZORPAY_PRICE_<PLAN>_PAISE.
- Prices are integers in paise (₹499 = 49900). No conversion rates exist anywhere in the code; nothing is derived
  from USD prices.
- The pricing cards (/pricing, upgrade prompts, /in) read the SAME numbers through GET /api/billing/catalog, so the
  displayed price is always the charged price. Before approval the catalogue returns no India prices at all.
- Checkout creates the Razorpay order server-side at that price; verify/webhook accept a payment only if amount and
  currency equal the order exactly (otherwise it is held as 'review' and unlocks nothing).

  Pro "Experience"  ₹999 per party            ₹1,499                  RAZORPAY_PRICE_PRO_PAISE=99900

Reasoning (assumptions — please challenge them):
- Keeps the US ladder shape (1 : 2 : 3.3 vs 1 : 2 : 3) so plan upgrades feel the same in both markets.
- Pro stays under ₹1,000, a strong psychological threshold for a one-off consumer digital purchase in India.
- Relative to the party: typical urban kids' birthday budgets in India are in the ₹10,000–₹50,000+ range; ₹299–₹999 is
  ~1–5 % of the event, comparable to the US ratio ($10–$30 on a $250–$1,000 party).
- Not a USD conversion: at ~₹85–90 per US$, $9.99 would be ~₹870. A straight conversion would price India out;
  a purchasing-power view puts a "$10 feeling" closer to ₹250–₹350.
- AI cost per party is small at these prices: the provider costs ≈ $0.005–$0.01 per request (docs/ai), i.e. a fully
  used Pro allowance (50 requests) ≈ $0.50 ≈ ₹45; Starter (10) ≈ ₹9.

Validation plan (cheap, reversible): launch with the recommended table for 30 days, watch the India funnel in
/admin → "Markets — US vs India" (party → checkout → purchase), then decide between keeping, lowering Starter to
₹199, or moving to the premium table. Prices are env values: changing them needs no code change, only a redeploy
(and existing purchases keep the amount they paid).


4. OPERATIONAL CONSIDERATIONS (need the owner / an accountant)
-------------------------------------------------------------
- GST: prices shown to consumers in India should be GST-inclusive. Whether MBP must register for GST (and at what
  turnover threshold) depends on the legal entity and where it is registered — confirm with a chartered accountant
  BEFORE going live. If registered, 18 % of each price is GST (₹999 incl. GST → ₹846.61 + ₹152.39 GST), and invoices/
  receipts must carry the required details (Razorpay can issue payment receipts; tax invoices are the merchant's
  responsibility). [NEEDS BUSINESS APPROVAL]
- Razorpay fees: a percentage per payment plus GST on the fee (check the current Razorpay pricing page for your
  account; UPI and card rates differ). Budget ~2–3 % of revenue.
- Settlement: Razorpay settles to an Indian bank account (T+2 typical for standard accounts).
- International cards: off by default on new Razorpay accounts. Keep them off at first — it also stops people
  relabelling a US party as "India" to buy at INR prices with a foreign card.
- Refunds: done in the Razorpay dashboard. A FULL refund automatically ends that party's plan (webhook
  refund.processed); a partial refund is recorded but keeps the plan (goodwill credits).
- Price arbitrage: a parent can set a party's country to India. Entitlements stay per party, so the risk is revenue
  (a US event paid at INR prices), not access. With international cards off, paying requires an Indian payment
  method. Monitor in the Markets report; revisit if it appears.
- Currency in reports: USD and INR revenue are reported separately and never added together.
